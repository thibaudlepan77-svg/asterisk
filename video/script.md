# Demo video script, Asterisk
Target length, two minutes forty. Slides plus voiceover, no camera.
Narration is written to be spoken, short sentences, no filler.

---

## 1. THE NUMBER  (0:00 - 0:20)
VISUAL, the contest header, 149,525 dollars in cash, 513 participants.
NARRATION.
I was ranking online contests by prize divided by entrants. This one topped
the list by a factor of five. A hundred and forty nine thousand dollars, in
cash, five hundred and thirteen people registered.

## 2. THE SAME PAGE  (0:20 - 0:40)
VISUAL, the disclaimer, repeated, with a counter reading fifteen.
NARRATION.
Under every prize tier on that same page, fifteen times, it says this is not
a cash prize, and that nothing can be exchanged or redeemed for cash. The real
cash was zero. Nothing was hidden. I had simply read the number and not the
page.

## 3. THE PROBLEM  (0:40 - 0:55)
VISUAL, three offer headlines, free, unlimited, no fees, each with a small
grey asterisk.
NARRATION.
This is not fraud and usually not even a lie. It is a number in one place and
its cancellation in another, with a scroll bar in between. Free that becomes
charged. Unlimited that becomes capped. No fees, until a fee.

## 4. WHAT IT DOES  (0:55 - 1:15)
VISUAL, real terminal output on the contest page, critical finding.
NARRATION.
Asterisk reads the offer and shows you both halves. The promise, and the
sentence that takes it back. Every line it prints is copied from the page. A
finding it cannot point at is dropped before you see it.

## 5. AND THE OTHER HALF  (1:15 - 1:30)
VISUAL, quiet conditions block, students only, auto renewal, non refundable.
NARRATION.
It also reports what no headline contradicts. Reserved to students. Renews by
itself. Non refundable. Nothing promised otherwise, and it binds you anyway.

## 6. THE AGENT  (1:30 - 1:50)
VISUAL, the architecture diagram, agent half highlighted.
NARRATION.
One page is not enough. The clause that costs you is usually behind a small
grey link. Built on Strands Agents, it ranks the links that could hold binding
conditions, follows the ones worth a request, and stops when another page would
add nothing.

## 7. THE FAILURE THAT SHAPED IT  (1:50 - 2:10)
VISUAL, side by side, licences against licenses, marked ALTERED.
NARRATION.
It had a tool to verify its own quotes. It called it. It passed. And its answer
still respelled the quote, and labelled it verified. So verification moved to
after the last token, on the text you actually read. An altered quote is now
shown as altered, next to what the page really says.

## 8. THE NUMBERS  (2:10 - 2:30)
VISUAL, the held out table, four labels.
NARRATION.
On forty pages drawn at random from thirteen thousand finished contests, pages
the rules were never written against, three labels are scored and the fourth
has no positive case to score. The one figure below one is the reference and
not the tool. Its single false alarm quotes a page that plainly asks for a
video, and the quote is in the readme.

## 8b. THE REFERENCE WAS WRONG  (2:30 - 2:48)
VISUAL, the 0.27 slide.
NARRATION.
One number came back bad, and it was the measurement. The first version of that
reference scored the tool at zero point two seven, and every one of its eleven
false alarms was a page that plainly asks for a video. It had been built from
the one wording its author had read. It was rewritten once, and deliberately
not a second time.

## 9. THE LIMIT I WILL NOT HIDE  (2:30 - 2:45)
VISUAL, the red warning block.
NARRATION.
Two pages in ten draw their prices in the browser. On those, a plain fetch sees
nothing, and reporting nothing found would be the worst thing this tool could
do. So it says so, loudly, and exits with its own code.

## 10. CLOSE  (3:00 - 3:12)
VISUAL, repository address, gallery address, MIT.
NARRATION.
Asterisk. Read the asterisk before you read the promise. MIT licensed, the
measurements are rerunnable, and its reports on eight pages you already know
are online.
